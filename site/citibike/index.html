Citi Bike Tides

Watching New York's bikes drain out of neighborhoods every morning and flood back every evening.

LivePython · AWS Lambda · S3 · CloudFront · CDKUpdates every 5 min
Open the map →

The itch

If I slept in, every bike near me was gone. If I rode home after work, there was nowhere to dock. It happened often enough that it clearly wasn't luck. I love data, so I wanted to see the pattern for myself: where the bikes go every morning, and where they all end up at night.

What it does

Every Citi Bike station is a dot on the map, colored by whether it gained or lost bikes over the previous hour. Press play and the whole day animates: Midtown and the Financial District soak up bikes in the morning, then the residential neighborhoods fill back up at night.

  • Live plays back today, minute by minute, up to a few minutes ago.
  • Average weekday shows the typical tide, built from past trip data.
  • Trucks is an experiment: spotting the overnight vans that rebalance bikes between stations.

How it works

Citi Bike publishes how many bikes are at every station. I save a copy every minute, then every five minutes I pack the latest hour into a small file the map can play back.

Snapshots land exactly on the minute, so every hour starts at :00 and the hourly gains and losses add up exactly. The builder runs just after each fifth snapshot (:02, :07, :12…), so the map is never more than a few minutes behind.

The raw snapshots are kept separately from the website, so the site can be torn down and rebuilt without losing any data.

What's next

Finishing the Trucks view. Bikes don't only move by riders: vans quietly shuffle them around overnight, and I want to see where.
